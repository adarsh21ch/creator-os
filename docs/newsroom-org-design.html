The shape of it

A newsroom, not a chatbot

You described a team, so I built it like a real newsroom: desks that run on a clock, a manager who assigns and reports, and a strict order of work. The order is the whole point — Intelligence finds it, Creative writes it, Production ships it, Performance judges it, and Performance writes back into Creative. That last arrow is what separates this from thirteen separate chat windows.

Most people who build this skip the last arrow. Then every script starts from zero and the tool stops being useful in three weeks. Here, the Playbook Keeper rewrites one file every week, and the Hook Writer and Scriptwriter read that file before they write a single line. The system gets sharper whether or not you do anything.

The scoreboard is not views

The goal you described is a cult, not a chart: an audience that recognises you, relates to you, and follows you off Instagram later — to YouTube, to a podcast, to a brand deal. Views do not measure that. So the Performance Desk is tuned to four numbers instead: follows-per-post (did this reel convert a stranger), saves (did they want it again), profile visits (did they want you, not the clip), and returning viewers (did the same person come back). A 400k-view reel that produced eleven follows is a failure here, and the system will say so.

One consequence worth building for: cults run on repetition. The Playbook therefore also tracks recurring assets — your catchphrases, your named series, your signature formats — and the Scriptwriter is instructed to reuse them deliberately rather than inventing a new personality every reel. Recognition is the mechanism.

The clock

What a day actually looks like

Your schedule already has content from 9 AM to 12. The newsroom is built around that block, not around itself. Everything the desks can do without you happens while you are asleep, so that at 9 AM you are choosing, not researching.

The roster

Every employee, and exactly what they hand over

An employee is defined by what lands on someone else's desk. If an agent's output isn't the input to a named next agent, it shouldn't exist. Here is the full staff.

Intelligence Desk

02:00–05:00 · unattended · 4 employees

Answers one question: what is worth talking about today. Runs before you wake up so the answer is already waiting.

I-01News Desk Analyst

Reads the newspaper and RSS list you give it, every night. Pulls only stories that touch your niche and discards the rest. Each survivor becomes a story card: source link, two-line summary, why it could travel, and how fast it will go stale.

Scores each on shareability — not on importance. An important story you can't make a 60-second reel about is a story for someone else.

I-02Influencer Watch

Watches your 40 handles, each tagged by you as right / left / neutral. Every new post gets logged: topic, format, the hook line, views, likes, comments, posted-at. Then you filter it the way you described — show me every right-wing account that touched this topic in the last 7 days, sorted by views.

It also decides which of their posts deserve a full teardown, and it does not use an absolute view threshold. A 50k account pulling 300k views is a far stronger signal than a 5M account pulling 1M. So an outlier is defined relative to that account's own 30-day median — roughly 3× their own normal. That is what surfaces a format that is genuinely working, rather than just surfacing whoever is biggest.

One honest limit: shares and saves on other people's posts cannot be obtained by any tool. Instagram gives those numbers only to the account that owns the post. The desk uses (likes + comments) ÷ views as the engagement proxy and applies it consistently, so comparisons between accounts stay fair even though the absolute number isn't the real one.

I-03Trend Scout

Not general Instagram trends — trends inside your niche, which is the only kind you can act on in time. It flags a format or an angle when three or more of your watched accounts use it within seven days. Three independent accounts converging is a real early signal; one viral reel is not.

I-04Deep Research Analyst

The only desk that runs on demand instead of on a clock. You say research this and it returns a dossier: verified facts with dates and numbers, primary sources you can open, what the other side argues, and a Risk line listing claims you should not make on camera.

This is the one that lets you speak on a topic with actual authority rather than repeating what another reel said.

Creative Desk

on demand · while you watch · 4 employees

Turns intelligence into a shootable reel. This is the desk you will actually talk to.

C-01Topic Planner

The bridge, and the most underrated job here. It takes everything the Intelligence Desk found plus your five pillars and returns tomorrow's menu: five topics, one per pillar, each with a why-now line and a confidence note. Fifteen raw stories are a burden. Five decided reels are a day's work.

C-02Hook Writer

Eight hooks per approved topic, every one built on your own formula: recognizable trigger word first, then a specific outcome, with the method withheld. It tags each hook wide reach or high intent so you pick deliberately instead of by feel.

C-03Scriptwriter

Writes in your voice, learned from 20–30 of your own reel transcripts. 60–90 seconds, Hinglish, talking head, a re-hook every 10–15 seconds, one built-in share line, one CTA, with pause and emphasis cues marked. You reply make it angrier, cut 15 seconds, more Hindi and it rewrites on the spot.

Script quality here is directly proportional to how many real transcripts you feed it. Five transcripts produce a generic AI script. Twenty-five produce something that sounds like you.

C-04Packaging Writer

Caption, first comment, hashtags, on-screen text, cover-frame text, and the YouTube Shorts title. Six different jobs that most people write as one — the cover frame stops the scroll, the caption earns the share, the title does search. Different words for each.

Production Desk

2 AI + 2 humans

You were right that AI can't edit video. So this desk's job is to make the human work as small and as delegatable as possible.

P-01Production Coordinator

Turns an approved script into two documents: a shoot card (setting, wardrobe note, props, any b-roll to grab) and an edit brief (cut points, where captions sit, where to zoom, music mood, target length).

The edit brief is the real unlock — it is written so a hired editor can finish a reel without calling you. That is what turns editing from your bottleneck into someone else's task.

P-02Publishing Scheduler

Holds the calendar and the slot times — derived from your own data, not from generic best-time-to-post advice. Tracks every reel through its states: Idea → Researched → Scripted → Shot → Editing → Scheduled → Published. Anything stuck more than two days goes into the Manager's brief as a blocker.

Performance Desk

daily pull · weekly teardown · 3 employees

The smallest desk and the one that decides whether any of this was worth building.

A-01Performance Analyst

Pulls your own post metrics properly through Instagram's official API — views, reach, likes, comments, shares, saves, and follows-from-post. For your own account these numbers are fully available and licensed; no scraping needed.

Then it answers the question you actually asked: which content earned followers, which earned shares, which earned saves, which earned comments. These are usually four different reels. Broken down by pillar, hook type, script length, and posting time.

It stops at which. Working out why is the Pattern Analyst's job; keeping the answer somewhere the writers will actually read it is the Playbook Keeper's.

A-03Pattern Analyst

Out of a hundred reels, ten outperform. This employee's only job is to work out why, structurally, and then let you fire that same structure at a completely different topic.

It takes each winner's actual transcript and breaks it into beats with timings — relatable open (0–4s) → contradiction (4–9s) → three escalating examples (9–40s) → the turn (40–55s) → share line — names the mechanism that makes it work, strips every trace of the original topic out of it, and files it as a numbered, reusable Format. The Scriptwriter then writes new topics into that beat structure instead of starting from a blank page.

The trap it is built to avoid: looking only at your winners. If eight of your ten hits open with sarcasm, that proves nothing until you check the flops — if forty of your ninety flops also opened with sarcasm, then sarcasm is not the cause, it is just how you talk. So a pattern is only promoted to a Format when it shows up in three or more winners and is meaningfully rarer in the losers. Everything else stays a hunch and is labelled as one. Almost everybody gets this backwards and ends up extremely confident about the wrong thing.

It also tracks format fatigue. A format that worked eight times starts dying somewhere around the tenth use, as your audience learns the shape before you do. Every Format carries a use count and a performance-per-use trend, so the desk can flag one for retirement or mutation before it drags you down rather than after.

And it runs in both directions. Pointed at the Influencer Watch desk's outliers, the same teardown tells you what is working on other creators' pages — their topic, their hook shape, their format, their structure — and files those as formats you can fire at your own topics.

Doing that to forty accounts naively would be very expensive, because a teardown needs the transcript, and getting a transcript means pulling the video and running speech-to-text. So it runs in two stages:

Stage one, nightly and cheap: scrape all forty accounts, numbers only — no video, no transcription. Stage two, selective and paid: only a post that beats its own account's 30-day median by roughly 3× gets its video pulled, transcribed and torn down. That is perhaps five to fifteen reels a week instead of six hundred, which is the difference between about ₹300 a month and about ₹15,000.

Borrowed formats are not proven formats, and the library keeps them apart. Anything torn out of your own winners is filed as Proven — it already worked on your audience. Anything lifted from another creator is filed as a Candidate, and stays a candidate until it has worked on your page twice. Mixing these two is how creators end up copying a format that was never going to transfer and concluding their content is broken.

The same forty-account data answers a second question for free: where the gaps are. If a topic is pulling heavy engagement across the left-wing accounts and no right-wing account has touched it in ten days, that is an opening, and the Topic Planner gets told.

A-02Playbook Keeper

Once a week it rewrites a single file — the Playbook — with what won, what died, and what changed. The Hook Writer and the Scriptwriter load that file as context before they write anything.

This is the compounding agent. Build everything else and skip this one, and in three weeks you have thirteen chatbots that never learned anything. Build this one, and month three is measurably better than month one without you doing anything extra.

What actually copies, and what doesn't

Worth being precise about, because "duplicate what works for them" is true of some layers and dangerous for others.

LayerTransfers?Why
Format / structureCopy freelyBeat order, pacing, where the contradiction lands, where the turn sits. This is mechanical and audience-independent. It is the single most portable thing in short-form.
Hook shapeCopy the shapeThe construction transfers; the trigger word does not. Swap in a word your audience recognises — their trigger word is tuned to their following, not yours.
TopicTest, don't assumeA topic that hit for a 2M account can die flat on your page, because their audience is not your audience. Treat a borrowed topic as a hypothesis, and give it a Proven format so at least one variable is controlled.
Voice / personaNever copyThis one is not a technical limit, it is the goal. You said you want people to follow you to YouTube and to a podcast later. Nobody follows the second version of someone else. Borrow the skeleton; the voice stays yours, which is exactly what the Scriptwriter's voice profile is there to protect.

The Manager

M-00 · the only one you talk to
M-00Chief of Staff

Runs the desks on schedule, chases what's stuck, and files one Daily Brief at 06:00 containing exactly six things:

1. What's trending today in your niche. 2. What's ready for you — topics, hooks, scripts waiting. 3. What's working, from last week's numbers. 4. What stopped working, which matters more. 5. What's blocked and needs a decision from you. 6. One question it wants answered.

You reply to the brief in plain Hinglish. It re-tasks the desks from your reply. That is the entire interface — you never need to open thirteen different screens.

Where you actually work

It is a website, not a chat window

Three separate things are easy to confuse, so to be exact about it: this chat is the workshop where the software gets built and decisions get made. The website is where you work every day — it holds the data, runs the schedules, and keeps the history. The Claude or Gemini API is the engine inside the website — the same intelligence, but running on a clock instead of waiting for you to type.

The reason it cannot stay in a chat is narrow and decisive: a chat cannot wake up at 2 AM. Everything the Intelligence Desk does depends on work happening while you sleep, and everything the Pattern Analyst does depends on a hundred reels sitting in a table you can query. Neither is possible in a conversation. You open a browser at 9 AM — on your phone or your laptop — and the work is already done.

Under the hood

How it's built, and what you switch in the admin panel

Same stack as your other apps, so nothing new to learn: React + Vite + TypeScript + Tailwind on the front, Supabase for database, auth, edge functions and the cron scheduler, deployed on Vercel.

The important architectural decision: an employee is a database row, not a code file. Their name, their instructions, their model, their schedule — all editable from the admin panel. That means you can hire a fourteenth employee, fire one that isn't earning its cost, or rewrite the Scriptwriter's instructions at 11 PM without anyone touching code.

The provider switch you asked for

Admin → Settings → AI Provider: pick Anthropic or Google, paste one key, hit Test. Keys are stored as Supabase secrets, never in the browser. Then each employee can override the default — which is how you keep the bill down: a cheap fast model does the scraping, tagging and classification work, and only the Scriptwriter and Deep Research Analyst get an expensive model. A running spend-per-employee meter sits next to each row, so if the Trend Scout costs ₹800 a month and you never read its output, you can see that and switch it off.

The Brand Brain

One shared file every employee reads before it does anything: your voice profile built from the transcripts, your five pillars, your hook formula, your positioning, and a banned-claims list. Update it in one place and all thirteen change behaviour at once. This is what stops the team from drifting into generic AI voice over time.

Before I start

What you paste in, and where it goes

You asked whether to hand this to me in chat or put it in the product. Put it in the product — you were right, and I have moved the intake screens to week one so they exist before you need them. Chat data dies when the session ends; product data is queried every night at 2 AM for as long as the tool runs, and it is the same screen a client would fill later.

Only one item is an exception, and only because it de-risks the rest.

  1. Three reel transcripts — in chat, nowNot thirty. Three. I need them before the pipeline exists so I can prove the voice profile actually sounds like you before you invest any time in the other twenty-seven. If three fail, the approach is wrong and we find out on day one instead of day ten.
  2. The other 27 reels — into the product, week onePaste the reel URLs into the Voice Training screen. It pulls the video, strips the audio, transcribes it in Hinglish, and adds it to your voice profile. Never do the download-convert-paste loop by hand thirty times.
  3. Your 40 influencer handles — into the productWatchlist screen, each tagged right / left / neutral. Handles, not links.
  4. Your newspaper and source list — into the productSources screen. Hindi and English both, whichever you actually read.
  5. Confirm your pillars — in chatMy notes have your five pillars as AI Tools, Trend-jack, Taarak Mehta format, Network Marketing, and Your Apps. But right-wing / left-wing influencer tracking and daily newspaper reading points at a political-commentary niche instead. This changes the Topic Planner and I need the answer before I write it.
  6. Your Instagram account type — in chatBusiness or Creator, and whether a Facebook Page is linked. Without that link, none of your own follow, share and save numbers can be read, and the Performance Desk has nothing to score.
  7. One AI key — into the productAnthropic or Gemini. This is the only non-optional key: it is the brain all thirteen employees run on. Apify, Instagram and speech-to-text get added later, desk by desk, as each one is built.
One thing worth saying

Build this for yourself first and resist turning it into a product for a few months. You already have Academy OS with a hundred-tenant target, and a Creator OS sold to other creators would be a second business competing for the same weeks. The org above happens to be multi-tenant-shaped, so the door stays open — but keep it shut until your own page proves the system works.
